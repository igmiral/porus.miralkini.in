01 — THE MAN BEHIND THE LEGEND

Who Was King Porus?

P

Porus, also known as Puru in some historical traditions, was an ancient Indian ruler who governed a territory in the Punjab region.

He is best known for confronting Alexander III of Macedon, commonly called Alexander the Great, at the Battle of the Hydaspes in 326 BCE. The battle took place near the Hydaspes River, identified with the modern Jhelum River in present-day Pakistan.

Although Alexander won the battle, ancient accounts describe Porus as a formidable opponent. His courage and leadership made a lasting impression on the Macedonian conqueror.

Much about Porus's early life, family and exact kingdom boundaries remains uncertain. The surviving accounts were written by Greek and Roman authors, rather than by Porus himself or his court.

02 — ROOTS OF A RULER

Early Life & Rise to Power

A life partly hidden by time

Very little reliable information survives about Porus's childhood, education or personal life. Ancient writers focused mainly on his confrontation with Alexander, leaving many details of his earlier years unknown.

He ruled in the north-western part of the Indian subcontinent, in a region shaped by rivers, agriculture, trade routes and competing political powers.

03 — THE PAURAVA REALM

The Paurava Kingdom

Porus ruled a kingdom in the Punjab region, between important river systems of the north-western Indian subcontinent. Its precise borders and administrative structure are not known with certainty.

01

Geography

The kingdom is associated with the region around the Hydaspes (Jhelum) and the Acesines (Chenab) rivers.

02

People & Society

The wider Punjab region supported settled communities, agriculture, local trade and established political traditions.

03

Military Strength

Ancient accounts describe Porus's forces as including infantry, cavalry, chariots and war elephants.

Historical note: Ancient geographical descriptions do not always match modern borders. The exact location and extent of Porus's territory remain debated.

06 — THE YEAR 326 BCE

The Battle of Hydaspes

The Battle of the Hydaspes was fought in 326 BCE near the Hydaspes River, now known as the Jhelum. It became one of the best-known battles of Alexander's campaign in the Indian subcontinent.

DATE326 BCE

The challenge of the river

Alexander's army faced Porus across a river that was difficult to cross, especially during the monsoon season. Ancient accounts describe how Alexander used a manoeuvre to cross at a different point, seeking to surprise Porus and gain a tactical advantage.

The clash of armies

Porus deployed a force that included war elephants. These animals could disrupt formations and frighten unfamiliar horses. The Macedonian army, however, had experience coordinating cavalry, infantry and disciplined formations.

The result

Alexander's forces defeated Porus. Ancient accounts describe Porus as having fought bravely and being captured or brought before Alexander after the battle. The details vary between sources.

08 — THE FAMOUS ENCOUNTER

How Did Porus Face Alexander?

Ancient writers describe Alexander asking Porus how he wished to be treated. Porus is traditionally remembered for answering that he should be treated like a king.

This exchange became one of the most famous parts of the Porus story. However, the exact wording is preserved through later historical narratives and should not be treated as a verified word-for-word transcript.

The popular dialogue is a traditional account, not a recording of the conversation.

09 — AFTER THE BATTLE

What Happened Afterwards?

Ancient accounts state that Alexander allowed Porus to continue ruling, and that his territory or authority was enlarged. This arrangement is often understood as a political decision to govern through a local ruler rather than remove him.

01

Porus remained a ruler

Traditional accounts describe Alexander restoring Porus to power after the battle.

02

A political alliance

Porus became an important local ally in the region under Alexander's wider campaign.

03

Alexander's advance stopped

After reaching the Hyphasis (Beas) River, Alexander's troops refused to continue farther east, and the army turned back.

11 — BEYOND THE BATTLEFIELD

The Legacy of Porus

Porus remains a memorable figure in the history of ancient India because his encounter with Alexander connected the political and military histories of two major regions.

His story has been retold in historical writing, popular culture and educational discussions. Over time, Porus has come to represent courage, resistance and the dignity of a ruler who faced a powerful invading army.

Yet his legacy is also a reminder of the importance of careful history. Ancient sources preserve valuable details, but they were written from particular viewpoints and sometimes disagree. Separating evidence from later legend helps us understand Porus more clearly.

13 — READING THE PAST

Historical Sources

Much of what is known about Porus comes from later Greek and Roman writers who described Alexander's campaigns. Their works are important, but they were not eyewitness records in every case and should be read critically.

Arrian

Anabasis of Alexander — one of the major surviving accounts of Alexander's campaigns.

Plutarch

Life of Alexander — a biographical account that includes episodes from Alexander's life.

Diodorus Siculus

Library of History — a broad historical work that preserves a version of the campaign.

Curtius Rufus

Histories of Alexander the Great — a Roman-era narrative of Alexander's life and conquests.

Research reminder: These authors differ in details. Numbers, speeches and dramatic scenes in ancient battle narratives should be treated with caution.

14 — QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Frequently Asked Questions

Who was King Porus?

Porus was an ancient Indian ruler associated with the Paurava kingdom in the Punjab region. He is best known for fighting Alexander at the Battle of the Hydaspes in 326 BCE.

Did Porus defeat Alexander?

No. Ancient accounts describe the battle as a Macedonian victory. However, Porus's resistance and leadership made a strong impression in the historical tradition.

Where was the Battle of Hydaspes fought?

It was fought near the Hydaspes River, identified with the modern Jhelum River in present-day Pakistan.

What made Porus's army special?

Ancient accounts highlight its war elephants, along with infantry, cavalry and chariots.

Did Alexander allow Porus to rule again?

Ancient sources report that Alexander allowed Porus to retain his position and authority after the battle, though details vary between accounts.

Did Porus really say “Treat me like a king”?

This is a famous traditional version of the encounter. The exact wording cannot be verified as a word-for-word historical quotation.

What happened to Alexander after the Hydaspes?

Alexander continued eastward until the Hyphasis River, where his troops refused to go farther. He then began the return journey.

Are all details about Porus historically certain?

No. Important parts of his story come from later Greek and Roman accounts. His exact dates, family background and kingdom boundaries remain uncertain.
